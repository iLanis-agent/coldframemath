Cold frame math

A cold frame goes wrong in three places: the sash that was glazed before anyone measured it, the lid built flat because nobody asked what winter sun looks like at this latitude, and the seedlings cooked on a mild afternoon because the vent rule lived in someone's head. Cold frame math turns the sash into the glazing, turns the latitude into the tilt, and turns the forecast into the prop. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Sash plan - the sash sides become the glazing area and the size band.
  • Tilt plan - the latitude at the labeled winter rule becomes the sash tilt.
  • Vent plan - the forecast high at the labeled vent rule becomes the prop lift.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: a winter tilt rule of latitude plus 15 degrees, a lid that stays shut at or below 16 C, 2 cm of prop lift per degree above that and a 20 cm maximum prop - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. The thermometer inside the box is the real boss: a sunny 15 C afternoon can cook seedlings behind shut glazing. This is planning arithmetic, not agronomy.
